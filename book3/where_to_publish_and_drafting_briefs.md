# Where to publish and deposit, and drafting briefs (former Appendices D and E), checked November 2026

## Appendix D — Where to Publish and Deposit

| Output | Where | Examples |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Preprints | Preprint servers by field | medRxiv (health), bioRxiv (biology), arXiv (quantitative and computational fields), SocArXiv (social sciences), regional servers |
| Landscape and bibliometric articles | Field journals; science-of-science and research-policy journals | The leading journals of your field; *Quantitative Science Studies*, *Scientometrics*, *Journal of Informetrics*, *Research Policy*, *Research Evaluation* |
| Reviews | Field journals; evidence-synthesis journals | *Systematic Reviews*, *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, *Campbell Systematic Reviews*, *JBI Evidence Synthesis* |
| Protocols | Registries and protocol journals | PROSPERO, OSF Registries |
| Datasets | General and field repositories | Zenodo, OSF, Figshare, Dryad, institutional repositories |
| Data papers | Data journals | *Scientific Data*, *Data in Brief*, *GigaScience* |
| Code | Code hosting with archiving | GitHub or GitLab, archived with a DOI on Zenodo or Software Heritage |
| Software papers | Software journals | *Journal of Open Source Software* |
| Policy briefs and reports | Institutional repositories; organisation websites | Your institution's repository, with a DOI where possible |
| Teaching materials | Open repositories | Zenodo, OSF, institutional repositories, with a CC BY licence |

Check each venue's scope, open access options, costs, review process and indexing before submitting.

## Appendix E — Drafting Briefs for Each Output

Each brief below starts the AI draft of Chapter 2's pipeline. Every brief carries the same rule: *use only the evidence pack; keep every number as given; do not add facts, references, quotes or numbers that are not in the pack.* The briefs for the policy brief, the abstract and the news story were tested in Chapter 4; the others have not yet been tested, and each needs the claim check like any draft. Book 2, Appendix D, shows how to test a prompt.

Test each prompt on your own material, check every claim and number, and record the version used.

**E1. Readers and outputs (Chapter 1)**

> *Here is a summary of my analysis and its main findings: [paste]. List the groups of people who could use these findings, what decision or task each group faces, and which type of output (brief, article, dataset, dashboard, story, course) would serve each best. Do not invent findings.*

**E2. Candidate gaps (Chapter 3)**

> *Here are counts from my landscape by topic, population, country and study type: [paste]. List candidate research gaps, name the kind of each gap (evidence, population, geography, method, translation, contradiction, time, leadership), and say what evidence would confirm or reject each. Do not claim that any study does or does not exist.*

**E3. Article title and abstract (Chapter 4)**

> *From this article blueprint: [paste], suggest five titles that state the question or main finding without exaggeration, and a structured abstract of 250 words. Use only numbers from the blueprint.*

**E4. Dataset README (Chapter 5)**

> *From this list of files and columns, and these methods notes: [paste], draft a README with: summary, authors, methods in brief, files, quality, limitations, licence, how to cite, contact. Do not describe any file or method not listed.*

**E5. Policy brief (Chapter 6)**

> *From these findings, with their uncertainty: [paste], write a two-page policy brief for [reader] facing [decision]. Include three key messages, two to four options with evidence and trade-offs, and a limits section. Use plain language. Do not recommend a single option. Do not add numbers.*

**E6. Portfolio review summary (Chapter 6)**

> *From these tables of a funder's grants, linked outputs and benchmarks: [paste], draft a two-page summary for the funder's board. Describe the portfolio; do not rank individual grantees; state match rates and limits.*

**E7. Press summary (Chapter 9)**

> *From this paper abstract and key result: [paste], write a one-page press summary for journalists. Use only claims made in the paper. Avoid causal language unless the study design supports it. Include a "what this does not show" paragraph.*

**E8. Dashboard update note (Chapter 10)**

> *Here are this month's indicators and last month's: [paste]. Write a five-sentence note on what changed. Flag any change that could be due to indexing delays. Do not interpret beyond the numbers.*

**E9. Workshop exercises (Chapter 10)**

> *From this teaching dataset description and these learning outcomes: [paste], write five exercises with answers, from easy to harder, each taking 10 to 20 minutes.*
