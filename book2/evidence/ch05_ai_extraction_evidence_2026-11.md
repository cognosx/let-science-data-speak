# Chapter 5 — AI data extraction: published evidence

Evidence checked in November 2026. These numbers will age; update this file as new studies appear. The book keeps only the summary.

| Study | What was tested | Result |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Jansen et al., 2025 (*Psychological Bulletin*) | 8 LLMs, 186 variables, 2,179 studies, 312,329 data points | Excellent accuracy for 12% of variables; unacceptable for 20%; accuracy varied most between variables, least between models |
| Bianchi et al., 2025 | Elicit against human reviewers, 20 randomised trials | Elicit's extraction deviated from the human one in 4.3% of cases; for intervention effects, 95% were only partly equal |
| Kataoka et al., 2025 | GPT-4o, randomised trials | Text fields (country, design, drug names) more accurate than numbers; 84.4% on one external set, 96.3% after adjusting the method |
| Mahmoudi et al., 2025 | ChatGPT, 60 data elements | 72% overall; 93% for explicitly stated settings; 50% for subjective components |
| ISPOR Europe, 2025 | ChatGPT, same prompt over five weeks | Accuracy varied over time with the same prompt and papers |
| Chen et al., 2025 (*Nature Communications*) | 4 LLMs on 12 biomedical NLP benchmarks | Fine-tuned models still beat zero-shot and few-shot LLMs on most extraction tasks; LLMs showed missing and invented information |
