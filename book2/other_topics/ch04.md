#### How the analyst did it

**The no-code path.** For each paper, the analyst needed to know whether the full text could be mined. He checked two things in Europe PMC: whether the record had a PMCID (free to read), and whether it carried the open access flag that allows text mining. In the website, the record page shows a "Free full text" link for the first and an "Open access" label for the second. He added two columns to his sheet: "free to read" and "free to mine". Of 191 papers, 63 had a PMCID; only 35 could be downloaded as full text for mining.

**The code path.**

```python
import requests
def mineable(pmcid):
    url = f"https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/{pmcid}/fullTextXML"
    return requests.get(url, timeout=60).ok
df["free_to_mine"] = df.pmcid.apply(lambda p: isinstance(p, str) and mineable(p))
```

**What the analyst reports.** "Of 191 papers, 63 were free to read in PubMed Central and 35 were open for text mining in Europe PMC (18%)." The two numbers stop a reader from assuming the analysis covered every free paper.
