#### the analyst: the edit that made 4,981 records

This error happened for real, in the run behind this book; we give it to the analyst. While writing his search into a script, he edited the query by hand. One line was copied twice by mistake, and a bracket moved. The search still ran and returned 4,981 records instead of 193: one bracket had changed the logic of the whole query. Nothing crashed. His check caught it: only 2 of the 4,981 titles contained "sp. nov." or "gen. nov.", against 191 of 193 before. **Write one check that says what your corpus should look like** (a word in most titles, a range of years, a likely size), and let the pipeline stop when it fails.

#### the analyst: rerun to see the trend move

the patent counts for the last two years will grow as applications are published. Her pipeline reruns the same query every six months, keeps each snapshot, and reports how much the recent years changed. Her landscape becomes a living report (Book 3), not a one-time picture.

#### How the analyst did it

**The no-code path.** the analyst keeps his search in a text file, never only in his browser history. When he changes it, he saves a new version with the date, and writes one line in his log: what changed and why. Before using any result, he looks at the count and at ten titles. A jump from 193 to 4,981 records would stop him at once, if he looks.

**The code path.** He makes the look automatic. After every search, the script checks what the corpus should look like, and stops if it does not:

```python
share = df.title.str.contains(r"\b(?:sp|gen|subsp)\.? nov\b", case=False).mean()
assert len(df) < 1000, f"{len(df)} records: check the search before going on"
assert share > 0.9, f"only {share:.0%} of titles describe new taxa: check the search"
```

When his edit broke the query, the first check would have failed on the count and the second on the titles (2 of 4,981). Nothing else in the pipeline would have noticed.

**What the analyst reports.** In his methods: the search, the date, the count, and the checks the pipeline ran. In his changelog: the broken edit, how it was caught, and the fix. Errors that were caught are evidence that the checks work.

#### How the analyst and the analyst did it

**the analyst** schedules her patent pipeline to rerun every six months, keeps each snapshot with its date, and adds a table to her report: applications per filing year, in each snapshot. The table shows readers how much the recent years have grown since the first count.

**the analyst** writes his access routes into his pipeline's configuration: the source, the date, and whether a key was used. Keys stay in environment variables, never in files, and a check scans every file for them before he shares the repository (Trap T30).
