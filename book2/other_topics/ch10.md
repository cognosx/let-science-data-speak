# Chapter 10 — From Landscape to Review: the method on other topics (moved from the printed book)

#### the analyst: is a review needed?

Before planning a systematic review of phage therapy, the analyst checks what exists. PubMed tags 73 records on phage therapy as systematic reviews or meta-analyses. Her landscape shows why few of them can pool effects: only 5 registered phage trials were phase 3 (and 2 more phase 2/3), and most of the clinical evidence comes from case reports and compassionate use. She chooses a scoping review of the clinical evidence, with a gap map by infection type and route of administration, and registers the protocol before searching.

#### the analyst: a review of methods, not effects

the question is practical: which culture conditions have recovered new respiratory species? A meta-analysis does not fit; a structured review of methods does. His extraction from full texts becomes the review's data table, and his gap map shows which specimen types and conditions have rarely been tried.

#### the analyst: the review that would age in months

the analyst considers a systematic review of LLM accuracy in evidence synthesis. Several already exist, and new models appear every few months. He chooses a living review with a clear update rule, and records the model version and date for every study.

#### How the analyst did it

**The no-code path.** the analyst searched PubMed for phage therapy with the filter "Systematic Review" and the publication type "Meta-Analysis": 73 records. She sorts them by date and reads the most recent ones first, asking for each: does it pool trial effects, or summarise case series? With few trials registered, she expects mostly the second. She then checks PROSPERO for registered reviews in progress on her infection of interest before deciding that her question is still open.

Her gap map is a grid in a spreadsheet: infections in rows (lung, urinary, bone and joint, wound, gut), routes of administration in columns (inhaled, intravenous, topical, oral), and the number of studies in each cell, with the study type in brackets. The empty cells are her candidate questions, which she tests with the eight questions of Book 3, Chapter 4 before claiming any of them.

**The code path.** `Entrez.esearch(db="pubmed", term='("Phage Therapy"[Mesh] OR "phage therapy"[tiab]) AND (systematic[sb] OR meta-analysis[pt])')` returns the same count, and the companion notebook draws the gap map from a table of included studies.

**What the analyst reports.** The review type, and why: "A scoping review, because the clinical evidence is mostly case reports and early-phase trials; only 5 registered phage trials were phase 3, and 2 more phase 2/3."

#### How the analyst and the analyst did it

**the analyst** writes his review question in the format of a methods review: which culture conditions have recovered new bacterial species from human respiratory samples, and which specimen types remain unexplored? His table from Chapter 5 becomes the review's extraction sheet.

**the analyst** writes the update rule for his living review before the first search: every six months, or whenever a major new model is released, with model names and versions recorded for every included study.
